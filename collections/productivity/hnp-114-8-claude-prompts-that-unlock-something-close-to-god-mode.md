# 8 Claude prompts that unlock something close to God Mode

[← All prompt packs](../../CATALOG.md) · **Productivity & Life Admin** · [Original post ↗](https://www.facebook.com/aniksingalcom/posts/there-is-no-god-mode-in-claude-but-these-8-prompts-unlock-something-close-to-it-/1512333516922707/)

## Prompts

Copy a prompt and replace the bracketed text with your own context.

### 1. The Expert Gap Extractor

```text
I want to understand {{TOPIC}} the way a 20-year practitioner understands it, not the way a textbook explains it. → Identify the 5 things experts know about this topic that beginners are almost never told → For each one explain: why it is not commonly taught, what believing the opposite costs a beginner, and how knowing it changes your approach → Then tell me the single most counterintuitive truth about this topic that took most experts years to learn → Do not explain what the topic is. Assume I already know the basics. Start where the textbooks stop.
```

### 2. The Top 1% Roadmap

```text
Build me a honest roadmap to reach the top 1% in {{SKILL}}. → Start by identifying what the top 1% can do that the top 10% cannot. That gap is what I am training for. → Break the roadmap into three phases: foundational leverage, deliberate edge-building, and reputation compounding → For each phase specify: the exact subskills to prioritize, what most people waste time on at this stage, the one daily practice that separates people who plateau from people who break through, and a realistic timeline → End with the single most common reason talented people never make it to the top 1% in this field despite years of effort → Rules: No motivational language. No generic advice. Treat me as someone who is already serious and wants the honest version.
```

### 3. The Decade Compressor

```text
Compress 10 years of hard-won knowledge about {{TOPIC}} into the most important things someone needs to know right now. → Structure this as lessons, not facts. Each lesson should be something a person typically only learns by making an expensive mistake or spending years in the field → For each lesson include: what most people believe instead, what the reality actually is, and the practical change in behavior this should produce → Then give me the three mental models that, if someone internalized them early, would compress their learning curve by years → End with the question about this topic that almost nobody thinks to ask but should.
```

### 4. The Industry Insider Breakdown

```text
Explain how the {{INDUSTRY}} actually works for someone who has never been on the inside of it. → Start with how money actually flows through this industry, not how it appears to flow from the outside → Identify the 3 power structures most outsiders do not see: who actually controls outcomes, who absorbs the risk, and who captures the majority of the value → Explain the unwritten rules that insiders follow that are never documented anywhere → Tell me what a smart outsider consistently gets wrong when trying to enter this industry → End with the one insight that, if an outsider understood it before entering, would give them a disproportionate advantage over people who learned it the hard way
```

### 5. The First Principles Reconstructor

```text
I want to understand {{TOPIC OR PROBLEM}} from first principles, not from received wisdom. → Strip away every assumption that is commonly accepted about this topic and identify which ones are actually load-bearing versus which ones are just inherited convention → Rebuild the logic from the ground up using only what can be directly observed or reasoned from evidence → Identify at least 2 conclusions that first principles reasoning leads to that conventional wisdom contradicts → Then tell me: what would someone have to believe to disagree with this first principles analysis, and is that belief defensible? → Rules: Do not cite what experts say. Reason from the evidence directly. Show your working.
```

### 6. The Second and Third Order Thinker

```text
I am considering {{DECISION OR ACTION}}. → Walk me through the first order consequences: what happens immediately and directly → Then walk me through the second order consequences: what happens as a result of those first order effects, including the responses of other people and systems → Then walk me through the third order consequences: what emerges from the second order effects that most people would not anticipate → Identify the consequence at any order level that is most likely to be the one I will regret not seeing in advance → Then tell me: what information would meaningfully change this analysis if I had it, and how would I go about getting it?
```

### 7. The Bias Auditor

```text
I am going to share my thinking on {{PROBLEM OR BELIEF}} and I want you to find every flaw in it before I act on it. → First identify every assumption I am making that I have not explicitly stated → Then identify which cognitive biases are most likely distorting my analysis: be specific about how each one is showing up in my thinking, not just naming them → Then steelman the strongest possible counterargument to my position → Then tell me: if someone who disagreed with me turned out to be right, what would that mean for my plan? → End with the single most important question I should answer before I move forward → Here is my thinking: {{PASTE YOUR THINKING}}
```

### 8. The Personal Operating System Builder

```text
I want to build a personal operating system for how I make decisions, manage my time, and allocate my energy. → Start by asking me the 5 questions you would need answered to build this properly. Wait for my responses before continuing. → Once I have answered, design a complete operating system built specifically around my answers. Not a generic productivity framework. A system that reflects my actual constraints, values, and goals. → Include: a decision-making filter for when I face tradeoffs, a weekly review structure that takes under 20 minutes, a rule for what I stop doing immediately, and a single metric I use to evaluate whether a week was successful → End with the one belief I currently hold about productivity or time that this system implicitly challenges → Rules: Generic frameworks like time blocking or the Eisenhower matrix are only acceptable if they are meaningfully adapted to my specific situation.
```
