# 9 Claude prompts for deeper research

[← All prompt packs](../../CATALOG.md) · **Learning** · [Original post ↗](https://www.facebook.com/altiamkabir/posts/need-better-research-with-claude-use-these-9-prompts-to-g%D0%B5t-deeper-cleaner-and-m/26600729819561345/)

## Prompts

Copy a prompt and replace the bracketed text with your own context.

### 1. Research a topic from zero to clear understanding

```text
Act as an expert researcher and teacher. I want to understand [TOPIC] from beginner to advanced level. First, explain it in very simple language. Then break it into the most important subtopics I need to know. For each subtopic, explain what it means, why it matters, and how it connects to the bigger picture. After that, give me a learning path in the best order to study this topic. End with the top misconceptions, common mistakes, and a short self test so I can check if I actually understand it.
```

### 2. Turn a broad topic into a research report

```text
Research [TOPIC] and create a structured report for me. Include an executive summary, key definitions, current landscape, major trends, important numbers or facts if available, leading companies or tools involved, opportunities, risks, and future outlook. Keep the writing simple but insightful. Where evidence is uncertain or incomplete, clearly say so instead of guessing. At the end, give me the 5 most important takeaways and 5 follow up questions I should research next to go deeper.
```

### 3. Compare tools, companies, or options properly

```text
Compare [TOOL A] vs [TOOL B] vs [TOOL C] for [SPECIFIC USE CASE]. Do not give me a shallow feature list. Instead, compare them across learning curve, speed, cost, quality of output, flexibility, integrations, reliability, ideal user type, biggest strengths, biggest weaknesses, and best use cases. Then create a simple decision table showing which one is best for beginners, teams, solo users, budget users, and advanced users. End with a final recommendation based on different scenarios rather than one generic winner.
```

### 4. Find the root cause behind a business problem

```text
I am facing this problem: [DESCRIBE PROBLEM]. Research the likely root causes behind it. Break the problem into possible internal causes, external causes, process issues, skill gaps, technology gaps, and market factors. For each possible cause, explain why it happens, what signs would confirm it, and what data I should collect to verify it. Then rank the causes from most likely to least likely based on the context I provided. Finally, suggest a practical action plan to test and solve the top 3 causes first.
```

### 5. Research a market before entering it

```text
I am thinking about entering this market: [MARKET OR NICHE]. Research it for me like an analyst. I want to understand target customers, main pain points, common buying triggers, competitor types, pricing patterns, content angles, demand signals, barriers to entry, and possible risks. Also identify what appears saturated and where there may still be room to stand out. Then give me a beginner-friendly summary of whether this market looks attractive, difficult, or crowded, and explain why. End with a list of smart next steps before I spend money or time here.
```

### 6. Research an audience deeply before creating content or offers

```text
Research this audience: [AUDIENCE TYPE]. I want to understand how they think, what they want, what they fear, what frustrates them, what goals they care about, and how they describe their problems in simple words. Organize the findings into pains, desires, objections, priorities, and buying motivations. Then suggest the content topics, hooks, lead magnets, offers, or services that would be most relevant to them. Keep everything grounded in realistic audience behavior and do not invent dramatic claims. End with a clear summary of how to communicate with this audience better.
```

### 7. Research a trend without getting fooled by hype

```text
Research the trend of [TREND]. Separate hype from reality. Explain what is actually happening, what is being exaggerated, which use cases appear genuinely useful, and which claims seem weak or unclear. Break your answer into real adoption, media hype, business value, technical limitations, and future potential. Then explain who should pay attention now, who should wait, and what signs would show that this trend is becoming more important over time. End with a short verdict: fad, early opportunity, long-term shift, or still too unclear.
```

### 8. Research a person, company, or product before a meeting

```text
Help me prepare for a conversation with [PERSON, COMPANY, OR PRODUCT]. Research their background, positioning, likely priorities, strengths, recent moves, possible challenges, and how they are probably thinking about growth or competition. Then turn that research into a practical briefing note. Include a short summary, important context I should know, likely opportunities to mention, sensitive areas to avoid, and 10 intelligent questions I can ask that make me sound prepared and thoughtful. End with a one paragraph strategy for how to approach the conversation effectively.
```

### 9. Build a research workflow, not just one answer

```text
I want to research [TOPIC] thoroughly, but I do not want one giant answer all at once. Create a step by step research workflow for me. Start by defining the research objective, the main questions to answer, and the assumptions that should be tested. Then break the work into stages such as background research, source gathering, comparison, analysis, synthesis, and final conclusions. For each stage, tell me what to ask, what to look for, what to document, and what would count as a useful output. End by giving me the exact first prompt I should use to begin stage one.
```
